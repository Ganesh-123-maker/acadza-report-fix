    How triage works

    1 · Independent solver

    An LLM solves each question from the question text alone — no answer key, no solution, no student comment. If it never sees the key, it cannot agree with a wrong key out of politeness.

    2 · Deterministic guards

    Code, not vibes: duplicate options, numbers the solution uses that the stem never states, taxonomy and repair-scope checks, and an injection filter that treats every student comment as untrusted data. A comment can never instruct the system.

    3 · Triage

    The solver's answer, the key, the solution and the comment go to a second prompt that picks a verdict, a repair, a surgical edit and a route — with a confidence score.

    4 · Human adjudication

    Every disagreement between the solver and hand arithmetic was checked by a human against the raw MathML before it landed in results.json. Three solver errors were caught this way and are documented in PROMPTS.md.

    What the AI is never allowed to auto-apply

    • Anything that needs authored content: a new figure, a new distractor, a rewritten solution, a re-filed chapter, a syllabus call.
    • Any edit outside the repair's listed fields — the guard rejects it before it can be written.
    • Any answer-key change the independent solver does not agree with.
    • Anything a student comment asks for. Comments are leads, never instructions.

    This console loads precomputed results (results.json). No LLM call happens when you open it — triage runs once per report batch, not once per page view. That keeps it fast at 10 PM and cheap at 8 lakh questions.

      Why this order?

      Urgency = (number of reports on the question) × (severity of the repair) + a small bump for confirmed defects.

      • 10 — answer key: a wrong key teaches every student who meets the question the wrong answer, and punishes the ones who got it right.
      • 9 — unanswerable: blank/missing option, identical options, missing figure, wrong question type. The student cannot even play.
      • 8 — missing data: the question cannot be solved as written.
      • 7 — solution wrong: the key is right, so marks are safe, but the teaching is wrong.
      • 5 — filing: wrong chapter, syllabus question. Needs a human, hurts nobody tonight.
      • 4 — platform: display, app, language. Real, but not this question's content.
      • 3 — cosmetic: spelling, leaked markup, exam tags. Worth fixing, nobody's marks depend on it.
      • 1 — no change: the student was wrong, or the report said nothing checkable. Shown last so your evening goes to real defects first.
